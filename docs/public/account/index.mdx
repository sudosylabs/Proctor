# Use Your Proctor Account

A Proctor <Term id="user">User</Term> is one account in one Institution. A
password, an external provider identity, and MFA are ways to prove that account;
they are not separate accounts and they do not grant academic membership or
permission by themselves.

## What Account Access Does Not Prove

Successful authentication establishes who is asking and how recently or
strongly they proved it. Proctor still reads current authority for the exact
action and resource. An <Term id="affiliation">Affiliation</Term>, Academic Unit
membership, Class membership, and <Term id="role-binding">Role Binding</Term>
remain separate durable facts.

This distinction explains two common outcomes:

- a person can sign in successfully but still be unable to open an Exam or
  administer a Class; and
- ending a membership or Role Binding can remove access without deleting the
  person's account or authentication methods.

## Ask for Help Safely

Give an administrator the time of the failed action, the server origin, the
safe error code, and the request ID when one is shown. Never send a password,
cookie, bearer credential, DPoP proof or nonce, Invitation claim, recovery code,
or Attempt credential. Proctor deliberately uses generic signed-out failures
where a more specific answer would reveal whether an account exists.
